The palette of experimental fruits

An attempt to quantify qualitative traits of plum-apricot hybrids

Although Floyd Zaiger wasn't the first to breed plums and apricots together, he made their offspring mainstream.

Apricots and plums are from the same Prunus genus and share the same number of chromosomes, so they can be pretty easily bred to each other. Zaiger carved a niche in the science and industry of experimental fruit breeding. He took a plum and an apricot and crossed them, crossed some of their offspring, and then crossed some more. In 1989, his company, Zaiger Genetics, filed their first patent for a plum-apricot hybrid: the "Flavor Supreme" pluot.

Since then, Zaiger Genetics has patented over a hundred pluots, as well as hundreds of other interspecies crosses of cherries, plums, apricots, nectarines, and peaches (see the nectaplum, or pluerry, or peacotum). These aren’t simple crosses that can be traced up a simple family tree: some pluots come from parents that are themselves crosses of crosses of crosses.

It’s not a very profitable industry. You’ve got to love fruit and its possibilities.

Like breeding dogs, the purpose of breeding fruits is to select for certain attributes: skin that’s tougher to withstand commercial shipping or more delicate; skin and flesh color; frost hours to befit different growing zones; blossom times to extend the fruit season; and of course, the most important factor of all: taste.

From 1993 to 2014, the Dave Wilson Nursery—which has exclusive rights to sell patented Zaiger pluots®—conducted blind taste tests of fruit varietals. The judges were retail nursery owners and employees, media personnel, radio personalities, garden writers, and members of the California Rare Fruit Growers association. They rated each sample by acidity, sweetness, flavor, and attractiveness.

Ten patented varietals have made it to the top of these taste tests. Although fruit yumminess is in the tastebud of the beholder, the varietals that consistently scored highest weren’t usually just the sweetest, but those with a complex interplay of tart and sugar.
